Frequently asked questions

The questions decision-makers ask most often before working together, answered openly. Your question is not among them? Then an intro call is the fastest route.

When does a test strategy assessment make sense?

Whenever you need a dependable picture of where you stand: ahead of a critical programme phase, after a painful release, when taking on new responsibility, or before you invest in tools, automation or additional staff. Within a few weeks the assessment shows where the risks and levers sit. And it delivers a prioritised 90-day plan you can start working with immediately.

Do you work strategically or operationally?

Both, quite deliberately. Strategy without implementation stays on paper; implementation without strategy stays patchwork. Depending on the situation I develop strategy and governance, support its introduction, or take on operational responsibility for a period, for example as interim test manager. What matters is what your programme needs right now.

Can you also stabilise running, critical projects?

Yes, that is one of my focus areas. Particularly when milestones are wobbling and defects surface late, the first step is an honest picture of the situation: which risks genuinely determine release and timing? What follows is clear priorities, working test and defect processes, and a communication structure that management and the customer can trust again.

Which industries is this consulting suited to?

Companies developing complex embedded, mechatronic or safety-critical systems, including automotive, medtech, mechanical engineering, robotics, aerospace and industrial automation. The methods transfer well because the underlying problems are similar: late defects, unclear maturity, difficult release decisions.

How quickly do first results appear?

First dependable statements usually emerge within a few weeks: a focused assessment delivers a clear picture with prioritised fields of action after two to four weeks. In acute situations, the first transparency on what is critical and what can wait can often be established within days.

Do you work remotely or on site?

Both, in whatever mix serves the programme. Analysis, workshop and escalation phases often benefit from being on site; ongoing steering, reviews and coaching work very well remotely. I adapt to your way of working and to where your teams are located.

Do you also support international teams?

Yes. I have worked repeatedly with international, cross-functional and distributed teams, in both German and English. That is precisely where clear governance pays off: shared criteria, consistent defect management and metrics that mean the same thing everywhere.

What sets you apart from classic test managers?

The perspective. Classic test management organises test activities. I use test and quality as a steering instrument for the programme: surfacing risks early, enabling decisions, aligning disciplines. That includes translating between management, development, test and quality, both technically and in communication.

How does quality become a competitive advantage?

By making things predictable. Knowing maturity and risks early means deciding faster and more reliably: releases hold, time to market stabilises, expensive late corrections and field issues decrease, customer trust grows. Quality then acts not as a cost centre but as the ability to keep commitments. And that is exactly what differentiates in the market.

What does release readiness mean in practice?

A pre-agreed, verifiable answer to the question: can we release with a clear conscience? That includes clear criteria such as risk coverage, open defects by severity, evidence status and residual risks. Plus dependable data and a decision process that allows for consequences. Release readiness replaces gut feel with the ability to decide.
